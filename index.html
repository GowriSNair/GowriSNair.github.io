Healthcare classification, 608,567 patient records

The best model for diabetes screening was the one that missed the fewest at-risk patients.

I cleaned the data, ran five hypothesis tests on known risk factors (all significant), then compared four classifiers. In screening, telling someone at risk that they are fine is the costly mistake, so I chose on recall. Gradient Boosting caught 86% of diabetic cases; the logistic regression baseline caught 60%.

Family history, weekly physical activity and age drove 86% of the model's decisions. I deployed it as an interactive prediction app.

NYC taxi trips, group capstone

Where a taxi trip starts says a lot about how far it will go.

Our team analysed 383,102 Yellow Taxi trips from 2023. I led the data cleaning, taking the raw trips down to about 321,000 valid records, and checked the analysis and model results before they went into the report.

Fares were predictable almost exactly (R² 0.99) from distance and duration. Trip distance was harder: airport and Queens pickups carried the most signal, and a neural network clearly beat the tree models. We also mapped demand, which peaks on weekday evenings from 5 to 8 pm.

Short-term rental market, Vancouver

Vancouver's Airbnb market splits into four kinds of listing, and guests reward the same few things in all of them.

I segmented 4,705 listings with K-Means and PCA, then scored 277,819 guest reviews for sentiment. Price barely correlated with any single listing feature, so the segments explain pricing better than the raw numbers do.

Cleanliness, comfort, location and host communication drove positive reviews; noise and inaccurate photos drove complaints. Flexible mid-range listings had the strongest sentiment.
